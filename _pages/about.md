Hi there! My name is Charlotte, and I'm a climate science PhD student at Columbia University and the Lamont-Doherty Earth Observatory. As a part of the extremes group, I work with [Radley Horton](https://www.radleyhorton.com/) researching the impacts of severe weather. I'm fascinated by anything related to hydroclimate, ranging from global heat waves to the daily streamflow of my hometown river, the [Gatineau](https://www.riverapp.net/en/river/5f2d2e4a48b5d83d685d6cdb). 

At Columbia, my work has mainly focussed on analyzing the impact of extreme heat on electricity demand, with a particular interest in the places where tradtional electricity-demand models fail. I am also researching the impact of non-temperature variables (like humidity, sunlight, or wind) on electricity demand. 

Before starting my PhD, I got an MS at Dartmouth College, working with Jonathan Winter in the [Applied Hydroclimatology Lab](https://ahcg.github.io/) on Northeast [extreme precipitaton](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10584-023-03545-w) and [streamflow](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1752-1688.13120). Before that, I got an honours BSc at McGill University, where I spent a year working with Bernhard Lehner in the [Global HydroLAB](https://wp.geog.mcgill.ca/hydrolab/) building a machine learning model to predict the probability of [river intermittence](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03565-5). Between my Master's and my PhD, I spent three years working for Environment and Climate Change Canada, first for Meteorological Service of Canada, then for the [Greenhouse Gas Reporting Program](https://publications.gc.ca/site/eng/9.816375/publication.html). 

In my free time, I love going for bike rides, swimming, hiking, reading, and writing!
