I am a climate science PhD student at Columbia University and the Lamont-Doherty Earth Observatory. As a part of [Radley Horton's](https://www.radleyhorton.com/) extremes group, I am an interdisciplinary researcher focussed on the impacts of severe weather. I am broadly interested in global hydroclimate, ranging from heat waves to flooding. Currently, my work is focussed on the impact of extreme heat on electricity demand, with a particular focus on places where traditional electricity-demand models fail. I am also analyzing the impact of non-temperature climate variables (i.e. humidity, sunlight, wind) on electricity demand. My previous work has focussed on precipitation and streamflow prediction in the Northeastern United States and identifying global intermittent rivers.

Before I came to Columbia, I earned an MS working in the [Applied Hydroclimatology Lab](https://ahcg.github.io/) at Dartmouth and an honours BSc at McGill University, where I was a part of of the [Global HydroLAB](https://wp.geog.mcgill.ca/hydrolab/).
